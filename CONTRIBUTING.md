# Contributing to VoiceLoop

Thank you for considering a contribution. VoiceLoop protects both the project
owner's rights and the rights of every contributor and third-party author.

## Rights and provenance

Submit only material that you created or that you are legally entitled to
submit. Do not include confidential information, patient or clinical data,
private transcripts, credentials, copyrighted datasets, copied code, images,
audio, fonts, or other material unless its provenance and license permit the
proposed use and redistribution.

If a change uses third-party material, identify the source, author or
rightsholder, version, license, and any required notice in the pull request.
Do not remove or overwrite an existing copyright or license notice.

AI-assisted contributions are welcome only when the contributor reviews the
result and can responsibly make the same provenance and licensing assurances.
Name the material AI tool in the pull request when it contributed substantive
code, text, or graphics. A tool-generated commit identity is not, by itself, a
legal determination of authorship or ownership.

## License for contributions

VoiceLoop does not require a copyright assignment. Copyright in a contributor's
original contribution remains with that contributor unless a separate written
agreement says otherwise.

By intentionally submitting a contribution for inclusion in VoiceLoop, you
agree that the contribution may be distributed under GNU AGPL-3.0-only, the
license used by the project, and you confirm that you have the right to grant
that permission. This is an inbound-equals-outbound policy; it does not grant
ownership of third-party material to Marcin Romanowski.

## Developer Certificate of Origin

Every commit should include a `Signed-off-by` trailer. The sign-off confirms the
Developer Certificate of Origin 1.1: <https://developercertificate.org/>.

Use Git's sign-off option when committing:

```text
git commit -s
```

The sign-off is a provenance statement. It is not a transfer of copyright and
is different from cryptographic commit signing.

## Pull requests

A pull request should:

1. explain the purpose and scope of the change;
2. list new third-party material and its license;
3. disclose substantive AI assistance;
4. exclude secrets and personal or clinical data;
5. preserve existing notices and attribution;
6. pass the project's automated checks.

Security vulnerabilities should follow [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md), not a public
issue. Questions about ownership, provenance, or licensing should be resolved
before a change is merged.
